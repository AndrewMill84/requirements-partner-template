# Requirements writing profile

## Reference and claim
Reference: ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, Issue 9, 15 January 2025.
Official information: https://www.asd-ste100.org/
Official standard: https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf
ASD overview: https://www.asd-europe.org/standards-specifications/simplified-technical-english/

This is an adapted profile for requirements, not a reproduction of the standard or a claim of full compliance. Obtain the official document for detailed checks. The standard and dictionary are not bundled. Confirm applicable editions when adopting the profile; do not silently change the reference edition.

## Local requirements conventions
- Use `must` for a mandatory obligation. Keep recommendations outside normative requirements.
- State one independently verifiable obligation per requirement.
- Name the actor, condition, action and observable result where applicable.
- Use active voice and short sentences. Aim for 20 words where practical; this is a local editing target, not a universal STE limit.
- Use a decision table when prose hides combinations or precedence.
- Use one term for one concept. Define necessary domain terminology in [the glossary](glossary.md).
- Replace vague expressions such as `appropriate`, `relevant` and `as necessary` with an explicit rule or reference.
- Where calculations or time-sensitive rules apply, specify dates, units, precision and rounding when they affect the result.
- Preserve meaning during language edits. Ask about ambiguity.
- Use the organisation's spelling convention consistently (e.g. British English or American English, recorded as a deliberate local choice in [project-context.md](../project-context.md)).

STE controls vocabulary, meanings and grammatical use. A glossary entry alone does not prove that a word satisfies the official technical-noun or technical-verb rules. A language check must consider the applicable dictionary and writing rules.

## Two reviews
1. Behaviour review: Are the requirements complete, confirmed and testable?
2. Language review: Are the sentences and terms clear and consistent?

Record full STE verification only if it actually occurred, including edition, reviewer or method, findings and deviations. Clear language does not prove complete business rules.
